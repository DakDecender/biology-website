Car Crashes: Understanding the internal body systems involved

Musculoskeletal System's use case in scenario-1:

The musculoskeletal system is the structural support system of the body. It helps maintain shape and holds organs in place. In addition, it includes bones, cartilage, ligaments, joints, muscles, connective tissues that add further support and help your overall mobility. In the scenario where a man needs to avoid a speeding car, the musculoskeletal system works along with the nervous system to carry out a rapid response. This occurs with the eyes detecting the car that is approaching, sensory neurons carrying that information toward the central nervous system (the brain and spinal cord), and interneurons in the brain relaying and processing the signal so the brain can recognize the danger. This information is then sent to the motor neurons which send electrical impulses to the skeletal muscles. This causes the muscles in the legs, arms, neck, and core to contract as they pull on bones through tendons. Therefore, enabling the man to quickly press the brake pedal and turn the steering wheel. Moreover, the core, back, and neck muscles also stabilize the muscles which prevents the driver from losing control.

Cardiovascular System use case in scenario-1:

The cardiovascular system plays an instrumental role in quick reactions during emergencies. When a man sees the speeding car, his brain signals the adrenal glands to release adrenaline, as the heart pumps oxygen‑rich blood faster to the muscles. Increased circulation fuels the legs and arms, enabling his movement to brake or swerve. At the same time, blood flows to the brain as it sharpens focus and decision‑making. By delivering oxygen and nutrients efficiently, the cardiovascular system ensures the body responds instantly, helping the man avoid collision and protect himself from harm.

Nervous System

What does it do?

When a man sees a speeding car approaching, the nervous system detects the danger, processes the information, and sends electrical signals to produce a rapid response. The central nervous system, made up of the brain and spinal cord, acts as the body's control centre by receiving and processing information. First, the eyes detect the approaching car, and sensory receptors in the eyes convert the visual information into electrical impulses. Sensory neurons then carry these impulses to the brain, which processes the information and recognises the approaching car as a danger. The brain sends electrical impulses through motor neurons to the skeletal muscles, causing them to contract so the man can brake or steer quickly and avoid the collision. Through this coordinated response, the nervous system enables the man to react quickly and safely avoid the speeding car.

  • The eyes detect the approaching speeding car.
  • Sensory receptors in the eyes convert the visual information into electrical impulses.
  • Sensory neurons carry the electrical impulses to the brain.
  • The brain processes the information and recognises the approaching car as a danger.
  • The brain sends electrical impulses through motor neurons to the skeletal muscles.
  • The muscles contract, allowing the man to brake or steer quickly and avoid the collision.

In this case, the reflex is a fast and coordinated response to danger. The eyes see the speeding car and inform the brain, which signals the muscles so the man can react quickly and safely avoid the collision.
